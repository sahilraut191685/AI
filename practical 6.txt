Practical 6: Water Jug Problem using Depth First Search (DFS)
Aim: To implement the Water Jug Problem using Depth First Search in Python.

Theory:

The Water Jug Problem is a classical state-space search problem in Artificial Intelligence. It involves two water jugs of different capacities and the objective is to measure a specific quantity of water using a set of permitted operations.

In this practical, two jugs are considered: Jug A with a capacity of 4 litres and Jug B with a capacity of 3 litres. Initially, both jugs are empty. The objective is to obtain exactly 2 litres of water in Jug A.

The problem is represented using states in the form of:
\[
(a,b)
\]

Where:
- \(a\) represents the amount of water in Jug A.
- \(b\) represents the amount of water in Jug B.
The possible operations are:
1. Fill Jug A.
2. Fill Jug B.
3. Empty Jug A.
4. Empty Jug B.
5. Pour water from Jug A into Jug B.
6. Pour water from Jug B into Jug A.
Depth First Search (DFS) is an uninformed search algorithm that explores one path as deeply as possible before backtracking. It uses a Stack data structure that follows the LIFO (Last In, First Out) principle.

A visited set is maintained to avoid revisiting previously explored states. The algorithm continues generating possible states until the required quantity of water is obtained.

Time Complexity:
\[
O(b^m)
\]

Space Complexity:
\[
O(bm)
\]

Where:
- \(b\) = Branching factor.
- \(m\) = Maximum depth of the search tree.
Conclusion: The Water Jug Problem is successfully solved using Depth First Search by exploring possible states and performing valid water transfer operations.
